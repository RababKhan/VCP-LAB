## 1. Step by step: BD-rate of faster against medium, BasketballPass

Why BD-rate? At the same QP, presets give different bitrate AND different quality, so you cannot compare one number.  
BD-rate asks: for the SAME quality (Y-PSNR), how many percent more (+) or fewer (-) bits does the test preset need?  
Inputs: the 4 QP points (24, 29, 34, 39) of each preset, from results/config_summary.csv (columns bitrate_kbps and y).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | 849.265 | 38.9292 | 975.853 | 38.4868 |
| 29 | 403.611 | 35.2511 | 462.458 | 34.8147 |
| 34 | 200.099 | 32.1590 | 222.839 | 31.6979 |
| 39 | 101.979 | 29.4942 | 111.966 | 29.0720 |

Step a - validity: 4 points per curve, PSNR rises with bitrate, and the two PSNR ranges must overlap by >= 50 %.  
         medium PSNR range 29.4942-38.9292 dB, faster range 29.0720-38.4868 dB  
         common range = 29.4942 to 38.4868 dB = 96% of the smaller range  -> valid  
Step b - for each curve, take log10(bitrate) as a function of PSNR and draw a smooth piecewise-cubic curve  
         through the 4 points (PCHIP interpolation).  
         medium : log10(kbps) = 2.9290, 2.6060, 2.3012, 2.0085;  area under the curve from 29.494 to 38.487 dB = 22.24825  
         faster : log10(kbps) = 2.9894, 2.6651, 2.3480, 2.0491;  area under the curve from 29.494 to 38.487 dB = 23.12419  
Step c - average log10 difference = (23.12419 - 22.24825) / (38.4868 - 29.4942) = 0.09741  
Step d - BD-rate = (10^0.09741 - 1) x 100 % = +25.14 %  
         Positive: faster needs MORE bits than medium for the same quality.
